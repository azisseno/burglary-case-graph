The running cost cannot break the business case. Even the worst case below is 2.5% of baseline. What decides the 15% target is whether detectives save about 45 minutes per case, half of the 90 minutes they spend today. That is the number to defend in the pitch and to measure in the pilot.

Where 45 minutes comes from: clustering means a detective works one series instead of three or four separate cases. If a four-case cluster takes 150 minutes of analysis instead of 360, that is 52 minutes saved per case. The pilot's audit log measures this directly: detective minutes per case, before and after.

Assumptions and sources

  • Volume and labor: 10,000 cases a year, detective at $70 an hour for 90 minutes, officer at $50 an hour for 240 minutes, $305 per case, $3.05M a year. From the Cracking the Burglary Backlog case packet, which states the data is fictional.
  • Claude API list prices, November 2026: Opus 5.5 at $4 input, $20 output, $0.20 cache read per million tokens; Sonnet 5.5 at $2, $10, $0.20; Haiku 5.5 at $0.10, $0.50. Claude on Amazon Bedrock is priced by AWS and will differ in GovCloud.
  • Tokens per case are prototype estimates. The Linker dominates because it reads the graph across several turns. Measure in week one of the pilot and replace.
  • Amazon Neptune: storage at $0.10 per GB-month and I/O at $0.20 per million requests are from the worked example on aws.amazon.com/neptune/pricing. The serverless rate of $0.1608 per NCU-hour is carried as an assumption verify in the AWS pricing calculator before the deck goes out.
  • Compute, object storage at $0.023 per GB-month, and operations are engineering estimates for about 30 cases a day. The GovCloud overhead is a planning allowance, not a published rate.
  • Support and maintenance at $60,000 a year and a $250,000 build are planning figures for a pilot team, stated so judges can challenge them.

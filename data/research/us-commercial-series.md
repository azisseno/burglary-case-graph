# US commercial burglary series, outside Washington State, 2019 to 2025

Compiled 2026-11-05 from public sources only. No suspect or victim names, ages or street addresses are recorded. Business names are replaced by scene type. "Not reported" means the cited source does not say.

## Scope notes

- Window is incidents in 2019 to 2026. Sections 1 to 7 cover 2023 to 2025. Sections 8 to 12 were added later and cover 2026 incidents. The ranking table below predates them; see the addendum summary at the end.
- Justice.gov, dea.gov, paloalto.gov and 12news.com blocked automated fetching. Claims from those sites come from search-result summaries only and are marked "secondary". They should be re-read in a browser before use.
- All incident tables copy dates and times as published. Where two outlets disagree, the table notes it.

## Ranking for a cross-case linking demo

| Rank | Series | Incidents | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

## Summary

Best for a cross-case linking demo:

1. **Rochester MN.** Incidents are dated, the same method repeats (masks, gloves, camera spray-painting, saws, e-bike), a vehicle and tire track link the first scene to the suspect, and later scenes link by method, bike and stolen vehicles. The public record also shows a human lead missed for three months, a useful "what should the system have flagged" story.
2. **Long Beach CA.** A short, clean timed table with block-level places and one shared entry method. It works well for a map and a time-gap view. The robbery rows are a good test of a system that must not over-link.
3. **New Orleans LA.** A single vehicle across six incidents in six days, then tools and clothing matched to video. This is the simplest vehicle-based link.
4. **Bexar County TX.** Good for property-recovery and phone-record evidence; needs date cleanup.

Midlothian, Phoenix and Palo Alto are weaker because the key incidents or evidence are not published.

# Seattle and Washington State burglary series, 2021 to 2026

Date range widened to include 2026 at the lead's request. Series 8 to 11 are the 2026 additions. A section on government published scene photos and surveillance stills is near the end.

Public sources only. Suspects, victims, ages and exact addresses are left out. Athletes and entertainers are labelled "pro athlete A to F" and "entertainer". The letters are this document's own labels, not the sources'. "Not reported" means the pages read do not say.

## Method and reliability notes

- Pages were read in full unless marked "search excerpt only".
- The Seattle Police Department blotter blocks scripted requests for its 2025 Jaws of Life post, so that post was read through a web fetch tool. King 5 and Capitol Hill Seattle returned HTTP 403 and were not read.
- Dates in articles that say only a weekday are left as the weekday.
- Where two outlets disagree, both versions are shown.
- Entry and evidence details are thin for several series. The Data gaps lines say exactly what is missing.

## Which series suit a cross case linking demo

**Best overall: series 1 (athlete and entertainer homes).** It has about ten dated incidents across several homes, a named vehicle description, surveillance stills, a recovery at a resale store, and a published statement of how the second defendant tied to the first. It also has realistic messiness: conflicting dates between outlets, an incident missing from one list, and an unconfirmed link between two defendants. That lets a prototype show both a confident link and a link that needs an analyst's review.

**Best for evidence variety: the 2018 supplemental series.** It is the only one with fingerprints, DNA, a plate or vehicle on camera, phone location data and a tool found on the suspect. It falls outside 2021 to 2025, so the lead has to decide whether to use it.

**Best for a commercial method signature: series 3 (Renton crash and grab).** Six dated incidents in five weeks, one repeated method and one vehicle. The U-Haul paperwork shows a physical document tying a vehicle to two earlier victims.

**Good for vehicle and recovery links: series 4.** Recovered cars connect incidents in different cities, and plate swapping shows the kind of detail a prototype should handle.

**Good for a hard case: series 7.** Several similar rammings with no stated police link. Use it to show the prototype suggesting candidate links without claiming certainty.

**Best 2026 addition: series 8 (Bellingham ring).** Two dated incidents tied by vehicles, with surveillance video, GPS tracker warrant, a traffic stop and a go bag search. It also links to earlier burglaries in two other counties. It parallels series 5, which shows how a prototype could test similar group signatures across years, but no source links the two groups.

**Clean small linking example: the 3 Feb 2026 SPD pair.** An explicit police statement that two incidents share a suspect and a victim, with a prior arrest match.

**Needs a full read before use:** series 9, which came only from a search snippet.

**Weak for incident level data:** series 2, 5, 6, 10 and 11. They have clear outcomes and some linking language but almost no per incident fields. Use them only as case level context.
